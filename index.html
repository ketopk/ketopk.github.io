🔍 What Is halowscope?

Every wireless device near you — your router, your doorbell, your neighbour's baby monitor — sends out invisible radio waves. Most of the time you never notice them. When your Wi-Fi slows down, though, the cause is often hiding in those waves: too many signals crowded onto the same channel, or a device interfering with the ones you care about.

halowscope makes those signals visible. Instead of a complicated engineering tool with dials and numbers everywhere, you get a clear, simple display that tells you what is happening on the 2.4 GHz band. Think of it as a pair of glasses for radio waves.

The radio measurements are collected by an ESP32-S3 board paired with a Wi-Fi HaLow (802.11ah) module from Elecrow. That small piece of hardware listens to the air and sends what it hears over a long-range, low-power Wi-Fi HaLow link. Your Windows computer receives that stream and draws it for you. The result is a spectrum analyzer you can set up on a desk, in a workshop, or anywhere you want to keep an eye on the wireless environment.

✨ What You Can Do With It

  • See every signal at a glance. A live graph shows the strength of radio energy across the 2.4 GHz band, so busy and quiet spots stand out immediately.
  • Spot interference. When a microwave, a wireless camera, or a crowded access point is causing trouble, halowscope helps you find it instead of guessing.
  • Choose a better channel. Before you move a router or add a device, look at the graph and pick the calmest area of the band.
  • Watch over time. Signals come and go. Leaving halowscope running shows you the patterns — noisy hours, quiet hours, and devices that switch on unexpectedly.
  • Learn by looking. Because everything is drawn in a simple, visual way, you do not need an engineering background to understand it.
  • Reach farther with Wi-Fi HaLow. The HaLow link between the sensor and your network is built for distance and low power, so the measuring point can sit far from your computer.

🖥️ Before You Begin

You do not need to be a programmer to use halowscope. You only need a few ordinary things in place:

  • A Windows computer running Windows 10 or Windows 11 (64-bit).
  • A normal home or office Wi-Fi network, so your computer can reach the device.
  • An Elecrow ESP32 HaLow module, powered on and connected to the same network.
  • An up-to-date web browser — Microsoft Edge, Google Chrome, or Mozilla Firefox.
  • About 200 MB of free space on your hard drive.

If the HaLow module has not been set up yet, that is fine — you can still download and open halowscope first, then connect the hardware when you are ready.

🧭 Your First Look

The first time halowscope opens, it looks for your HaLow sensor on the network. Give it a moment. Within a few seconds you should see the graph begin to move as live readings arrive.

  1. Wait for the connection. A small status line at the top tells you whether the sensor has been found.
  2. Watch the graph fill in. Higher peaks mean stronger radio energy at that spot in the band. Flat, low areas are quiet.
  3. Point at anything you like. Hovering the mouse over the graph shows you a plain description of what is happening at that point.
  4. Let it run for a few minutes. Signals change constantly, so the picture becomes more useful the longer you watch.

There is nothing to configure to get started. Default settings work well for most homes and small offices.

🛠️ If Something Does Not Work

The graph stays empty

Check that the ESP32 HaLow module is powered on and connected to the same network as your computer. Then close halowscope and open it again. It looks for the sensor each time it starts.

Windows blocks the program

Open the file from your Downloads folder rather than from inside a compressed archive. If Windows still hesitates, use More info → Run anyway.

The window looks strange or very small

Close halowscope, then right-click it and choose Properties → Compatibility → Change high DPI settings. Turning on the option to override scaling usually fixes unusual display sizes on high-resolution screens.

Your antivirus asks a question

Allow the program to run. If it is placed in quarantine, restore it from your antivirus history and add the folder to the allowed list.

💡 Tips for Better Readings

  • Place the sensor away from metal cabinets and thick walls; radio waves have trouble passing through both.
  • Keep the sensor at least a metre away from your router if you want to measure very weak signals.
  • Run halowscope at different times of day to build a picture of when the band is busiest.
  • Use the results to move a router or a wireless camera to a less crowded spot in the band.
  • Because the HaLow link travels far, you can put the sensor in a shed, garage, or far corner of a building while you watch from indoors.
